Representation and intrinsic dimension

Each excerpt has one representation per model and layer: its last non-padding token’s hidden state. ESS, MLE, and local FCI estimate dimension from its neighbours in a separate 15,000-excerpt reference set. These are query-neighbourhood estimates, rather than per-token annotations or direct measures of linguistic complexity.

ESS
Expected simplex skewness: a local dimension estimate based on simplex geometry.
MLE
A local maximum-likelihood dimension estimate based on nearest-neighbour distance ratios.
Local FCI
The study’s pointwise adaptation of the fractal-correlation estimator. It should not be equated with the original dataset-level estimator.
Percentile
Average rank divided by the available evaluation population, separately within each model, layer, and estimator. Filters do not change this denominator.

Text, tokens, and neighbourhoods

The 46 linguistic predictors aggregate over each excerpt. UD word tokens differ from model subword tokens; definitions state the relevant denominators. Lexical density is displayed as a percentage of its stored fraction. Frequency controls use the shared recorded Pythia-tokenizer proxies.

The locality audit sampled 500 queries. All 500 are available in the explorer. Radius, distance, source composition, and lexical similarity describe the selected query’s neighbourhood; they are not properties of an individual word.

Six of the 2,000 geometric evaluation queries have no released linguistic profile. Their texts and recorded geometry remain available. One Pythia early-layer FCI estimate is unavailable. Missing values remain unavailable. Token-level scores, reference-neighbour texts, and new-text inference are not supplied.

Paper figures report dataset summaries, including 2,000-query aggregates. Plots include only excerpts with the measurements required for the selected axes. Associations do not establish causality.
